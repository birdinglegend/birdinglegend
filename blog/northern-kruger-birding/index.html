a Guide to Birding in Northern Kruger

Punda Maria • Pafuri • Shingwedzi

Northern Kruger National Park is one of South Africa's premier birding destinations. Its vast Mopane woodlands, towering baobab trees and lush riverine forests create habitats for several sought-after species that are difficult to find elsewhere in the park, making it a must-visit region for both dedicated birders and wildlife photographers.

After years of guiding in Kruger, I finally set out on my first solo birding adventure through Punda Maria and Pafuri in search of Northern Kruger's speciality birds, including Pel's Fishing Owl, Racket-tailed Roller, Arnot's Chat, Böhm's Spinetail and Crested Guineafowl.

In this guide, I'll share my journey through the northern reaches of the park while also providing practical advice on where to find these target species, the best birding roads to explore, seasonal conditions to expect and the lessons I learned along the way.

Before we dive into the guide, you can join me on the journey in the video below as I explore Punda Maria and Pafuri in search of Northern Kruger's most sought-after bird species.

Why Bird Northern Kruger ?

Northern Kruger offers a birding experience unlike anywhere else in the park. While the southern regions are renowned for their abundance of wildlife and high visitor numbers, the far north rewards patience with quieter roads, diverse habitats and several speciality birds that are rarely encountered further south. For many birders, this is where Kruger truly begins to feel wild.

From the ancient baobab trees around Punda Maria to the riverine forests of Pafuri and the winding Luvuvhu River, Northern Kruger supports a remarkable variety of habitats. Each one attracts its own unique birdlife, making the region one of South Africa's premier birding destinations.
